First post in what I hope becomes a long-running public work journal. The premise is simple: I write things down so I can search them again later.

> Not about being authoritative. About having a record.

## Why a public journal

Most of what makes cloud security work — actual production reality — lives in private Slack threads, post-mortem docs, and that one teammate's notebook. None of it is searchable, shareable, or survives a team change.

This is my attempt to put some of it on the open web. Short notes on:

- **Cloud security news** — what's happening in the industry, major breaches, vendor advisories, compliance changes
- **Technology trends** — how cloud security is moving (shift-left, runtime detection, AI in the SOC, etc.)
- **AI & agentic engineering** — from prompt to agent, and the security implications
- **Tooling & workflow** — small tools that save time, useful commands, config tricks
- **Incident post-mortems** — short, technical write-ups after a bad day
- **Short opinions** — takes on CVEs, viral blog posts, or industry shifts

## What you'll find here

:::note
This is **not** a tutorial series. Posts are written fast and published when they're useful — usually unpolished. I optimize for _grep_, not readability.
:::

Typical post format:

- Field note from a real investigation (with redacted screenshots)
- Small tool or script that saved an hour
- Incident retrospective (with the parts that are actually useful)
- Short opinion on a CVE or policy change

## Questions people ask

**"Why mix Malay and English?"**
Because that's how I think about technical things. A lot of terms have no good translation, and forcing it makes things awkward. Cloud computing is English by default — IAM, RBAC, CSPM, drift, posture. Try translating _"entitlement drift detection"_ and see what you get.

If you're not used to BM mixed with technical terms, don't worry — the English terms usually have enough context to be clear.

**"Is it Malay-only posts?"**
No. Mixed. Malay for things I want to reach Malaysians (especially Gen Z entering the industry), English for things that are more global or already a standard reference in the technical space.

**"How often do you post?"**
No fixed schedule. Publish when there's something _worth sharing_ — minimum once a month, maybe more if there's an incident or interesting tool.

**"Can I share / repost?"**
Yes. Nothing is locked. Take it, remix it, credit if you want.
