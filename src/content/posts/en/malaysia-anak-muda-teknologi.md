> Personal note — written in English to reach wider, and hope more of us write about tech in our own ways.

## Introduction

Every time someone mentions **cloud computing**, **AI**, or **cybersecurity** — the names that come up are usually from Silicon Valley, London, or Singapore. Rarely from KL, JB, Penang, or Kuching.

But on the ground, reality is different.

Malaysia **doesn't lack talent**. Plenty of young people are building systems, writing code, and running infrastructure used by companies globally — they just rarely write about it. Plenty are quiet.

This post is about them.

## Who I'm talking about

Not one group from a specific university or startup. I mean anyone in Malaysia who chose to **learn tech seriously**:

- **Developers** who build products quietly, solo, without waiting for permission
- **Sysadmins** who keep infra running so it doesn't crash in the middle of the night
- **Security engineers** who spend hours reading CloudTrail logs to protect user data
- **AI researchers** who experiment with LLMs to solve local problems — not generic problems from the West
- **Community contributors** who write tutorials in Malay, answer questions on Discord, and never ask for payment

They all exist. But our industry rarely sees or talks about them.

## Signs I'm noticing right now

### GitHub and Hugging Face are getting more active

Search `site:my` on GitHub — you'll find production-grade projects, not just university assignments. Contributors from Malaysia are growing, becoming more visible.

### Local AI initiatives are getting more relevant

Projects like **ILMU** and national AI initiatives show we can build our own foundation models — with our data, for our problems, in our language.

### Young people are **building now**, not just **using**

The trend of MicroSaaS, serious side projects, and OSS contributions from local community is visible. Very different from ten years ago.

### Grassroots communities are getting more active

DevFest, hackathons, local cloud & security Discord servers, weekend meetups — not all glamorous, but talent is there, support is there. What's missing is visibility and industry structure to match it.

## What you can do right now

Not national schemes. Personal action, solo:

| Do what                                                   | Why                                                                           |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Write one technical note in English**                   | Documentation in a language you're comfortable with makes explanation clearer |
| **Open source your repo**                                 | One more data point showing Malaysia isn't passive                            |
| **Contribute to someone else's project**                  | Translate docs, answer issues, fix typos — all counts                         |
| **Teach someone new**                                     | Teaching = most effective way to solidify your own understanding              |
| **Stop apologizing for writing about tech in your style** | Language isn't a barrier — language is a bridge                               |

## About AI and where we're headed

Large language models, autonomous agents, data pipelines — all mostly being built by large companies in the US and China. This means:

- Training data has certain biases
- Languages that are "less profitable" get ignored
- Local problems rarely become a priority

:::caution
If we only **use** AI and don't **build** it, we'll always be behind in decisions about how the technology shapes our own society.
:::

That's why Gen Z Malaysia needs to invest in deeper skills — not just using ChatGPT API, but understanding how models are built, data trained, systems distributed.

## Closing

I didn't write this post to say Malaysia has already won. Not yet. There's still a gap in ecosystem, investment, and international exposure.

But I write to remind myself: **I'm not alone**. Plenty of others are building, learning, trying.

> This generation isn't a generation that **waits for opportunity**.
> It's a generation that **creates opportunity** — even if sometimes just for itself first.

If you read this and you're learning tech in Malaysia right now — keep going. Your place is here.
